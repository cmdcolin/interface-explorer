G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) are the cell's most common sensors. They
sit in the cell membrane and answer to hormones, neurotransmitters, many drugs
and, in the nose, smells. When a receptor catches its signal outside the cell,
it changes shape inside and grabs a G protein, which passes the message on.
The part of the G protein that does the grabbing is its Gα subunit: the end of
one helix, α5, pushes up into a pocket that opens on the receptor's inner face.

BioHackathon Europe 2026 Project 9 picked {p.complexes} experimental
structures (cryo-EM and X-ray) of receptors for dopamine, serotonin,
adrenaline and histamine, each caught holding a G protein; we added one smell
receptor, OR51E2. In each structure we took the residues of each protein within
5 Å of the other, the contact surface, and placed on them the missense
variants (single amino-acid changes) that ClinVar collects from clinical
genetic testing.

**{p.gAlphaPathogenic} variants classed as pathogenic land on these contact
surfaces, and all are on the G protein: {p.gnasPathogenic} on Gαs,
{p.alpha5Pathogenic.length} of them on its α5 helix.** The receptor side has{' '}
{p.receptorPathogenic || 'none'}.

How to read the figure above:

  (the rest of the G protein is left out).
- Blue: residues within 5 Å of the other protein.
- Red and orange balls: residues with a pathogenic or an uncertain ClinVar
  variant.
- Left, <a href={href(`gpcr/${p.featured.pdb}/`)}>the dopamine D1 receptor

## The tip of Gαs

<Html html={p.gnas ? surfaceMap(p.gnas, p.gnasLabels) : ''} />

Gαs, from the gene GNAS, is a known disease protein: its variants cause
pseudohypoparathyroidism, in which the body stops answering to parathyroid
hormone. The map shows where along Gαs those variants fall relative to the
receptor contacts.

Bars: how many of the {p.gnas?.structures} Gαs structures put a receptor within
5 Å of each residue. Marks above: ClinVar pathogenic (red) and uncertain
(orange). The strip below the axis marks Gαs's helices and strands by the
common Gα numbering (CGN; Flock et al., Nature 2015), whose labels name the
same position in every Gα: G.H5.25 is the 25th residue of α5 in Gαs, Gαolf or
Gαi alike. The pathogenic variants by CGN element are{' '}
{p.gnasPathogenicByElement}. Outside Gαs: {p.otherPathogenic}. So one α5
position carries a pathogenic variant in two Gα genes.
AlphaFold DB has no complex of Gαs with a GPCR, so this surface comes from
experiment alone.

## The receptor side

Why the gap between the two sides? Partly it is ClinVar: GNAS is a classic
inherited-disease gene, so clinics test it and report what they find, while
most amine receptors are not. The Project 9 receptors' contact residues have{' '}
{p.uncertain} variants of uncertain significance and {p.benign} benign ones,
mostly from gene-panel testing. Some GPCRdb positions have variants in more than one receptor, but{' '}
{p.variants} variants over {p.positions} numbered positions would produce that
by chance, so we have not ranked them as leads.

The Gαs surface above comes from experiment alone, but where AlphaFold DB has a
matching complex it agrees: its DRD5–Golf complex covers {p.drd5Recall}% of the
receptor residues DRD5 uses against Gs in the cryo-EM structure, and its GPCR
complexes of Gαi1 cover {p.gnai1Recall}% of what the {p.gnai1Structures} Gαi1
complexes use.

## Gαolf and the olfactory receptors

Smell (olfactory) receptors do not use Gαs but a near twin, Gαolf, from the
gene GNAL. No experiment has caught a smell receptor holding Gαolf, but
AlphaFold DB predicts such pairs: GNAL complexes with {p.olfactory.length}{' '}
olfactory receptors ({p.olfactory.join(', ')}). Its confidence in each
contact, the ipSAE score, is {p.ipsaeRange.join('–')}, just above the{' '}
{t.ipsae} cutoff we use, so these predictions need checking.

OR51E2 is the one smell receptor with an experimental structure, holding
mini-Gs, an engineered Gαs, so it can check them. Below, "finds" (recall) is
the share of the experiment's contact residues the prediction also puts in
contact, and "on it" (precision) the share of the prediction's contact
residues that the experiment confirms.

OR51E2 bound to propionate with mini-Gs
(<a href={`https://www.rcsb.org/structure/${p.or51e2.pdb}`}>{p.or51e2.pdb}</a>,
cryo-EM, {p.or51e2.resolution} Å; Billesbølle et al., Nature 2023) tests the
receptor side of those predictions: AlphaFold DB's
OR51E2–{p.or51e2.partner} complex covers {p.or51e2.recall}% of the{' '}
{p.or51e2.residues} receptor residues within 5 Å of Gα, and {p.or51e2.precision}%
of the residues it predicts are among them.

### The Gαolf side

The mini-Gs is an engineered Gαs, but Gαs and Gαolf are{' '}
{p.or51e2.golf.identity}% identical and their α5 helices end alike (QYELL). Mapped onto Gαolf by sequence, the{' '}
{p.or51e2.golf.experimental} mini-Gs residues within 5 Å of OR51E2 test the other
half of the prediction. AlphaFold DB's OR51E2–Gαolf complex puts{' '}
{p.or51e2.golf.predicted} Gαolf residues against the receptor, and{' '}
{p.or51e2.golf.shared} of them are among those {p.or51e2.golf.experimental}: it
finds {p.or51e2.golf.recall}% of the experimental surface, and{' '}
{p.or51e2.golf.precision}% of what it predicts is on it. It misses only residue{' '}
{p.or51e2.golf.missed.join(', ')}.

ClinVar has {p.gnalVariants.length} variants of uncertain significance where
AlphaFold DB puts Gαolf against an olfactory receptor. Three GNAL variants are
worth placing:

- **L380R**, on α5 like the Gαs variants above: L380 ({p.golfCgn[380]}, Gαs{' '}
  {p.gnasCounterpart(380)}) is{' '}
  {p.golfReach[380]} Å from OR51E2 in {p.or51e2.pdb}, so it touches the receptor
  in the structure and in the prediction. It is the one to look at first.
- **K376Q:** K376 is the residue the prediction misses. Its Gαs counterpart at{' '}
  {p.golfCgn[376]}, {p.gnasCounterpart(376)}, is {p.golfReach[376]} Å from OR51E2 in {p.or51e2.pdb}; AlphaFold DB puts
  K376 against OR2A1 and OR2J3 instead.
- **D227N**, the dystonia 25 variant, is not a receptor contact. Its Gαs
  counterpart at {p.golfCgn[227]}, {p.gnasCounterpart(227)}, is {p.golfReach[227]} Å from OR51E2 in {p.or51e2.pdb} and{' '}
  {p.drd5Golf.reach[227]} Å from DRD5 in {p.drd5Golf.pdb}. AlphaFold DB has it
  against Gβ and the adenylyl cyclases ADCY2, ADCY3 and ADCY4, and within 5 Å
  of DRD5 in its DRD5–Gαolf complex, which {p.drd5Golf.pdb} does not bear out.
  GNAL's disease links are to dystonia; see [GNAL's page](../../gene/GNAL/).

GNAL's other predicted GPCR complexes use the same face:

- The other olfactory receptors
  ({p.or51e2.golf.olfactory.partners.join(', ')}) cover{' '}
  {p.or51e2.golf.olfactory.recall} of the {p.or51e2.pdb} surface, with{' '}
  {p.or51e2.golf.olfactory.precision} of their predicted residues on it.
- The {p.or51e2.golf.others.partners.length} other GPCRs, DRD5 and the trace
  amine receptors among them, cover {p.or51e2.golf.others.recall} but predict
  larger interfaces, only {p.or51e2.golf.others.precision} of which is on it.
- DRD5's own Gs structure ({p.drd5Golf.pdb}) agrees: AlphaFold DB's DRD5–Gαolf
  complex covers {p.drd5Golf.recall}% of its Gαs contacts mapped onto Gαolf,
  and {p.drd5Golf.precision}% of the {p.drd5Golf.predicted} residues it
  predicts are among them.

So on OR51E2, the Gαolf face AlphaFold DB predicts is the one a Gs mimic uses,
despite an ipSAE just over the threshold. A Gαs structure cannot test the{' '}
{100 - p.or51e2.golf.identity}% of Gαolf that differs from Gαs.
