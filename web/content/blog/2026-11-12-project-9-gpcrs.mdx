The interface atlas maps where human disease proteins touch their partners, from
AlphaFold DB's predicted complexes and from experimental structures, and which
ClinVar missense variants fall on those surfaces. For Project 9 it covers the
{p.complexes} receptor–G protein complexes in DataSet1. Three places to start,
depending on what you work on:

- **Alignments and activation (msa2d):** the [complexes on the GPCRs
  page](../../gpcr/#complexes), where every receptor contact and variant now
  carries its GPCRdb generic number.
- **Olfactory receptors:** [GNAL](../../gene/GNAL/), the Gαolf gene, and its
  predicted complexes with olfactory and trace amine receptors.
- **3D viewers (iCn3D):** each complex on the GPCRs page opens in a 3D view
  beside the genome.

## One numbering for everyone

The team agreed on GPCRdb's generic numbering, so the GPCRs page now uses it.
Each receptor residue within 5 Å of Gα is labelled with its GPCRdb number, e.g.
[HTR1A](../../gpcr/#HTR1A)'s R225C, R225H and R225S all sit at 5.68x68. That
covers {p.numbered} of the {p.contacts} contact residues; the other{' '}
{p.contacts - p.numbered} lie in loops GPCRdb leaves unnumbered and are labelled
{p.loops.join(' or ')}. Hover a complex's residue count for the full list.

The msa2d pages count Gα contacts within 4 Å, so our sets should be a slightly
larger superset of yours on the same structures. If yours are not, one of us has
a mapping bug, and we would like to know.

## Gαolf and the olfactory receptors

Olfactory receptors signal through Gαolf, encoded by GNAL. AlphaFold DB has
predicted GNAL complexes with {p.olfactory.length} olfactory receptors
({p.olfactory.join(', ')}) and three trace amine receptors (TAAR2, TAAR5 and
TAAR9). None is an OR4, so these do not test the OR4 models directly, but they
do show the surface Gαolf offers an olfactory receptor.

ClinVar has {p.gnalVariants.length} variants of uncertain significance on that
surface:

L380 is the second-to-last residue of Gαolf, at the tip of the α5 helix that
inserts into the receptor's cytoplasmic cavity, so an arginine there is the one
to look at first. Two things keep this modest:

- GNAL is a disease gene because of dystonia (DYT25), through dopamine D1-type
  signalling in the striatum, not because of smell. Several of these variants
  were reported for dystonic disorders.
- The olfactory receptor complexes have ipSAE {p.ipsaeRange.join('–')}, just
  above the 0.6 threshold for a confident interface. V204E and L380R also
  contact DRD5, ADRB1 and the TAARs: this is the face Gαolf turns to every
  receptor, not one specific to olfactory receptors.

## OR51E2: an olfactory receptor in experiment

To test those predictions, we added one structure that is not in DataSet1:
[OR51E2](../../gpcr/#OR51E2) bound to propionate with mini-Gs
(<a href={`https://www.rcsb.org/structure/${p.or51e2.pdb}`}>{p.or51e2.pdb}</a>,
cryo-EM, {p.or51e2.resolution} Å; Billesbølle et al., Nature 2023). Its{' '}
{p.or51e2.residues} receptor residues within 5 Å of Gα all carry GPCRdb numbers,
so they line up with the amine receptors. AlphaFold DB's
OR51E2–{p.or51e2.partner} complex covers {p.or51e2.recall}% of them, and{' '}
{p.or51e2.precision}% of the residues it predicts are among them: the predicted
Gαolf face of an olfactory receptor matches the one seen with a Gs mimic,
despite its borderline ipSAE.

The mini-Gs is an engineered Gαs, not Gαolf, so this checks the receptor side of
the prediction, not the Gαolf residues. ClinVar has {p.or51e2.variantCount}{' '}
variants of uncertain significance on OR51E2's side of the interface:{' '}
{p.or51e2.variants}.

## Where AlphaFold DB can be checked against experiment

Its DRD5–Golf complex covers {p.drd5Recall}% of the receptor residues DRD5 uses
against Gs in the cryo-EM structure, and its GPCR complexes of Gαi1 cover{' '}

## What we are not claiming

The receptor side of these complexes carries {p.uncertain} variants of uncertain
significance and {p.benign} benign ones, mostly from gene-panel testing. Several
generic positions are hit in more than one receptor, which is what{' '}
{p.variants} variants spread over {p.positions} numbered contact positions would give
anyway, so we have not ranked them as leads.

## What would help

- **Olfactory receptor group:** whether your OR4 models put the same residues
  against the G protein as OR51E2 does here; the GPCRdb numbers make that a
  column-by-column comparison.
- **msa2d:** your 4 Å receptor–Gα contact lists, to check against ours.
- **iCn3D:** your interaction export for the same complexes.
