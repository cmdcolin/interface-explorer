We mapped every confident AlphaFold DB predicted complex of a human disease
protein back to the genome, codon by codon, and asked which ClinVar missense
variants land on an interface. Within the same protein, the odds of landing on
one are **{odds(e.all).toFixed(1)}×** as high for a pathogenic variant as for a
benign one.

## It is not just folded versus disordered

Interfaces lie in folded regions, where pathogenic variants concentrate anyway.
Restricting the comparison to folded residues, then to folded residues on the
surface, leaves the effect standing: among exposed residues the odds ratio is
{odds(e.orderedSurface).toFixed(1)}×.

### Conservation explains only part of it

Pathogenic variants favour conserved bases, and interfaces are conserved.
Comparing variants only within the same quarter of conservation across 100
vertebrates lowers the odds ratios above but leaves most of the effect; the
[conservation post](../2026-11-12-interfaces-are-conserved/) has the detail.

<Html html={forestPlot(matchedRows(s))} />

Mantel–Haenszel odds ratios across proteins; lines are 95% intervals from
resampling proteins, and the dashed line marks no difference. Hover a row for
the number of proteins behind it.

## Caveats

Only {percent(s.withPartner, s.proteins)} of disease proteins have any predicted
partner, and the complexes are predictions: an interface variant is a lead, not a
mechanism. The method is on the [About](../../about/) page.
