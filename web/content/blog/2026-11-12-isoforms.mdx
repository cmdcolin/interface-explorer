A gene can make several proteins by splicing, and an isoform that skips an exon
loses whatever that exon contributed. AlphaFold DB now models UniProt's
alternative isoforms, so each one can be checked against the predicted
interfaces of the canonical protein.

## APP695, the neuronal isoform of APP

APP695 (P05067-4) has no Kunitz inhibitor domain, so it lacks most of APP's
predicted interfaces with the proteases KLK11, KLK14 and prostasin. APP751
(P05067-8) keeps the domain and lacks none.

None of APP's {app.count} ClinVar pathogenic missense sites, at residues
{app.first}–{app.last}, is in these interfaces, so this example is
about interfaces only. Each gene's isoform page says whether any of its
pathogenic sites is an interface residue.

## Across the disease genes

Across {isoforms.genes.toLocaleString()} disease genes and
{isoforms.alternativeIsoforms.toLocaleString()} modelled isoforms,
{isoforms.lackingInterface.toLocaleString()} isoforms lack half or more of a
partner interface, and {isoforms.genesLackingPathogenic.toLocaleString()} genes
have an isoform missing a residue with a pathogenic variant. In
{isoforms.genesPathogenicAtInterface.toLocaleString()} genes a pathogenic site
is itself a predicted interface residue. The [isoforms](../../isoforms/) page
lists every gene, starting with those that lose the most pathogenic sites, and
can be sorted by pathogenic sites at an interface.
