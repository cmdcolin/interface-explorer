Every interface in the atlas is a prediction. For {all.pairs.toLocaleString()}
predicted pairs, the PDB has an experimental structure holding both proteins, so
the prediction can be checked.
In {all.touching.toLocaleString()} of them the two proteins touch, and there the
predicted interface covers a median **{share(all.recall)}** of the experimental
one.

## It is not memory

AlphaFold was trained on the PDB, so agreeing with structures it has seen would
prove little. {unseen.touching.toLocaleString()} of the pairs had no structure
deposited by {s.validation.cutoff}, the cutoff of its training set. For those the
median is {share(unseen.recall)}, and
{percent(unseen.recovered, unseen.touching)} have at least half of their
experimental interface predicted, against
{percent(all.recovered, all.touching)} of all pairs.

## In both directions

- Of the residues in an experimental interface, a median {share(all.recall)} are
  in the predicted one.
- Of the predicted residues the structure resolves, a median
  {share(all.precision)} are in the experimental interface
  ({share(unseen.precision)} for pairs with no structure before the cutoff).

Each gene page names the structure its partners were checked against.

## Caveats

- One structure is compared per pair: among those covering nearly the most of
  both proteins, the one with the fewest chains.
- {(all.pairs - all.touching).toLocaleString()} pairs share a PDB entry in which
  the two proteins do not touch. They are subunits of rings and large
  assemblies, such as the Sm rings, the exosome and the spliceosome, that are
  not neighbours there. Contacts made only through crystal symmetry are also
  missed.
- A pair first deposited after the cutoff can still resemble an older structure
  of related proteins.
- Only pairs with a structure can be checked, and they are the well-studied ones.
