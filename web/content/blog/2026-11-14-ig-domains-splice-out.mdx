An immunoglobulin-like (Ig) domain folds on its own, so splicing can remove one
whole and leave the rest of the protein intact. If an antibody or CAR binds that
domain, the isoform has no epitope for it.

Each map below shows a protein's Ig-like domains from UniProt, numbered from the
N-terminus, and the isoforms that lack one. Red bars mark the domains that
clinical antibodies bind.

## CD33

CD33 has {cd33.domains} Ig-like domains. The isoform CD33m lacks the first, the
V-type domain.

<Html html={igCase('CD33')} />

The V-type domain is encoded by exon 2, and a common variant, rs12459419, makes
skipping that exon more likely. The domain is where gemtuzumab ozogamicin and
the P67.6 antibody bind
([Lamba et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5549451)). In the
Children's Oncology Group AAML0531 trial, gemtuzumab lowered relapse risk only
in patients with the CC genotype. A smaller study of a related conjugate found
no link to the genotype
([Stanchina et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6704571)), so
the effect is not settled.

## CD22

CD22 has {cd22.domains} Ig-like domains. UniProt lists {cd22.isoforms.length}
isoforms that lack at least one whole. CD22-alpha lacks domains 3 and 4, which
exons 5 and 6 encode.

<Html html={igCase('CD22')} />

[Zheng et al. (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9780083) found
that the isoform skipping exons 5 and 6 is in 16 of 18 diagnostic childhood
leukaemias and almost absent from normal B cells. It reaches the cell surface
and works as a protein. What it costs depends on the antibody:

- **HA22 CAR:** binds an epitope encoded by exon 5, and cells with only this
  isoform resisted it.
- **m971 CAR:** binds domain 7, which the isoform keeps, and killed these cells
  about as well as full-length ones
  ([Ereño-Orbea et al., 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8353475/)
  place its epitope there).
- **Inotuzumab ozogamicin:** binds the N-terminus, so these cells stayed
  sensitive.

Skipping exon 2 is a different problem. Exon 2 holds the start codon, so those
isoforms make no detectable protein and the map can't show them. Forcing exon 2
skipping cut CD22 to about a seventh and made cells resistant to inotuzumab. One
inotuzumab non-responder's blasts expressed mainly such isoforms.

Epratuzumab binds across domains 2 and 3
([Ereño-Orbea et al., 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8353475/)),
but the paper did not test it. UniProt also lists an isoform that lacks domain
1, P20273-5, which nobody has tested.

## Why it happens

- **CD33:** an inherited variant favours the shorter isoform from birth.
- **CD22:** the domain 3–4 isoform is already in most leukaemias at diagnosis,
  so a domain 3 binder has a gap before treatment starts. Exon 2 skipping can
  rise further at relapse.

Which cells make an isoform, and what sets that, is what the protein models
cannot tell us.

## What this does not show

- **Expression.** UniProt says an isoform exists, not when or where it is made.
- **Multimers.** Each map is one chain. Whether an isoform still joins its
  partners, or sits in the synapse a CAR makes, needs the assembly. Neither CD33
  nor CD22 has predicted interfaces in this atlas, so that is the next step.
- **Epitopes.** The red bars are whole domains from the cited papers. An
  antibody's contacts can cover part of a domain, or two.
