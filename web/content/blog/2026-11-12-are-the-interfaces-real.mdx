Every interface in the atlas is a prediction. Two checks say most of them are
real: experimental structures, where they exist, and evolution, which has kept
the predicted surfaces more conserved than the rest.

## Against experiment

For {all.pairs.toLocaleString()} predicted pairs, the PDB has an experimental
structure holding both proteins. In {all.touching.toLocaleString()} of them the
two proteins touch, and there the predicted interface covers a median
**{share(all.recall)}** of the experimental one.

Peripherin-2 and ROM1 are a pair AlphaFold could not have seen. They pair in the rims of photoreceptor
discs, and variants in [PRPH2](../../gene/PRPH2/?partner=ROM1) cause

pair, <a href={`https://www.rcsb.org/structure/${rom1.experiment.pdb}`}>{rom1.experiment.pdb.toUpperCase()}</a>,
came out in 2022. It puts {rom1.experiment.experimental} residues of PRPH2
against ROM1, and the prediction has {rom1.experiment.shared} of them, with
{rom1.experiment.predicted - rom1.experiment.shared} more of its own. On that
interface ClinVar has {count('pathogenic')} pathogenic and {count('uncertain')}
uncertain missense variants.

## Against evolution

Only the well-studied pairs have a structure. For the rest, conservation across
UCSC's 100 vertebrates is a check that needs none. For each confident complex we
compared the mean phyloP of its interface codons with that of the protein's
other folded, surface-exposed residues at no confident interface, matched for
exposure and pLDDT. The interface is more conserved in
**{percent(r.all['interface more conserved'], r.all.complexes)}** of
{r.all.complexes.toLocaleString()} complexes, by a median
{r.all['median difference']} phyloP units.

Share of complexes whose interface is more conserved, by ipSAE. Hover a row for

Any compact patch of folded surface might stand out like that. So we ranked
each interface among patches of its size from the same protein's surface at no
confident interface, one centred on each such residue, and ranked a random patch
the same way in the interface's place to learn what chance gives. The interface
beats the median patch in
**{percent(pa['above the median patch'].interface, pa.complexes)}** of
{pa.complexes.toLocaleString()} complexes, against
{percent(pa['above the median patch'].fake, pa.complexes)} for the stand-in, and
beats 95% of its patches in
{percent(pa['above 95% of patches'].interface, pa.complexes)}, against
{percent(pa['above 95% of patches'].fake, pa.complexes)}.

The share rises with ipSAE, as it would if the more confident predictions are
more often right. It is not just conserved domains: ranked only among patches
centred within 25 Å of its middle, the interface beats the median one in
{percent(near['above the median patch'].interface, near.complexes)} of
{near.complexes.toLocaleString()} complexes, against
{percent(near['above the median patch'].fake, near.complexes)} for a stand-in.

Conservation cannot vouch for the partner, though: AlphaFold reads the same
alignments, and could dock a wrong partner, such as a paralog, on a real
conserved site.

## Caveats

- One structure is compared per pair: among those covering nearly the most of
  both proteins, the one with the fewest chains.
- {(all.pairs - all.touching).toLocaleString()} pairs share a PDB entry in which
  the two proteins do not touch: subunits of rings and large assemblies, such as
  the Sm rings, the exosome and the spliceosome. Contacts made only through
  crystal symmetry are also missed.
- A pair first deposited after the cutoff can still resemble an older structure
  of related proteins.
- phyloP measures DNA, not protein: a codon can be conserved for splicing, and a
  free third position lowers a codon's mean.
- Surface residues at no confident interface may touch partners AlphaFold DB did
  not model, which makes the conservation comparisons conservative. Patches are
  the residues nearest a point, so they are rounder than many real interfaces,
  and the patch test covers {pa.complexes.toLocaleString()} of the
  {r.all.complexes.toLocaleString()} complexes.
