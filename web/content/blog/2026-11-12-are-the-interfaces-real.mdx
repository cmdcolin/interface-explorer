import { gene, summary } from '../../lib/data.ts'
import { href } from '../../lib/site.ts'

export const s = summary()
export const all = s.validation.all
export const unseen = s.validation.unseen
export const share = x => `${Math.round(100 * x)}%`
export const rom1 = gene('PRPH2').partners.find(p => p.gene === 'ROM1')

Every interface in the explorer is a prediction. Here is how they compare with
experimental structures where those exist; for the rest,
[conservation and gnomAD](../2026-11-13-evolution-and-people/) are checks that
need no structure.

## Experimental structures

For {all.pairs.toLocaleString()} predicted pairs the PDB holds a structure of
both proteins, and in {all.touching.toLocaleString()} of them the two touch.
There the prediction covers a median **{share(all.recall)}** of the
experimental interface, and {share(all.precision)} of the predicted residues
the structure resolves are in it.

AlphaFold was trained on the PDB, so the {unseen.touching.toLocaleString()}
pairs with no structure deposited by {s.validation.cutoff}, its training
cutoff, are the fairer test. Their median coverage is {share(unseen.recall)}.

PRPH2 and ROM1 are one such pair: their first structure,{' '}
<a href={`https://www.rcsb.org/structure/${rom1.experiment.pdb}`}>{rom1.experiment.pdb.toUpperCase()}</a>,
came after the cutoff. It puts {rom1.experiment.experimental} residues of PRPH2
against ROM1, and the prediction has {rom1.experiment.shared} of them. Each gene
page names the structure its partners were checked against.

## Caveats

- One structure is compared per pair: among those covering nearly the most of
  both proteins, the one with the fewest chains.
- {(all.pairs - all.touching).toLocaleString()} pairs share a PDB entry without
  touching, mostly subunits of rings and large assemblies.
- A pair first deposited after the cutoff can still resemble older structures
  of related proteins.
