import { gene, summary } from '../../lib/data.ts'
import { href } from '../../lib/site.ts'

export const s = summary()
export const all = s.validation.all
export const unseen = s.validation.unseen
export const share = x => `${Math.round(100 * x)}%`
export const rom1 = gene('PRPH2').partners.find(p => p.gene === 'ROM1')

To check a predicted interface, we take a PDB structure holding both proteins
and count how many of its contact residues the prediction also names. Pairs
with no structure can be checked against
[conservation and gnomAD](../2026-11-13-evolution-and-people/) instead.

## Experimental structures

The PDB holds both proteins for {all.pairs.toLocaleString()} predicted pairs,
and they touch in {all.touching.toLocaleString()}. There the prediction covers
a median **{share(all.recall)}** of the experimental interface, and
{share(all.precision)} of its resolved residues lie in that interface.

AlphaFold was trained on the PDB, so the fairer test is the
{unseen.touching.toLocaleString()} pairs first deposited after its
{s.validation.cutoff} cutoff. Their median coverage is {share(unseen.recall)}.

PRPH2 and ROM1 are one such pair: their first structure,{' '}
<a href={`https://www.rcsb.org/structure/${rom1.experiment.pdb}`}>{rom1.experiment.pdb.toUpperCase()}</a>,
came after the cutoff. Of its {rom1.experiment.experimental} PRPH2 residues
touching ROM1, the prediction has {rom1.experiment.shared}. Each gene page
names the structure its partners were checked against.

## Caveats

- Each pair is checked against one structure: of those covering nearly the
  most of both proteins, the one with the fewest chains.
- {(all.pairs - all.touching).toLocaleString()} pairs share a PDB entry without
  touching, mostly subunits of rings and large assemblies.
- A pair first deposited after the cutoff may still resemble older structures
  of related proteins.
