AlphaFold DB's predicted complexes, which the rest of the explorer is built on,
pair only human proteins. The PDB, though, has experimental structures of
SARS-CoV-2 proteins bound to human ones, some of them disease proteins with
ClinVar variants. Where does a viral protein touch one, and what does ClinVar
say about the residues it touches?

Of the {v.summary['pairs in one PDB entry']}
pairs that share a PDB entry, {v.summary['pairs that touch']} touch, across{' '}
{v.genes} genes, and {v.summary['touching pairs with a classified variant at the contact']}{' '}
have a classified ClinVar missense variant on the contact: {v.pathogenic}{' '}
pathogenic, {v.uncertain} uncertain and {v.benign} benign.

## Three worth a second look

**ISG15 and the papain-like protease.** PLpro strips ISG15 from host proteins,
and the crystal structure of the inactive protease with ISG15 puts{' '}
{v.byGene('ISG15')?.residues.length} ISG15 residues against it. ClinVar's
variants in ISG15 are all reported for ISG15 deficiency, a Mendelian
susceptibility to mycobacterial disease, and{' '}
{v.byGene('ISG15')?.variants.length} of them sit on that surface. These are
variants in the protein PLpro removes, with no known selection by the virus. The
structure shows they sit on the residues PLpro contacts.

## What does not touch

These pairs share entries but do not touch, so co-occurrence alone would have
listed them:

- **nsp1 and the ribosome.** {v.ribosomal.length} ribosomal disease proteins,
  including the Diamond–Blackfan anemia genes RPS19, RPS26 and RPS10, sit in the
  same entries as nsp1. None touches it. What nsp1 does touch in those entries,
  besides the rRNA, is {listed(v.nsp1Neighbours)} — the proteins around the 40S
  mRNA channel, none of them a disease protein in UniProt.
- **SLC6A20 and SLC6A19 with spike.** Both share entries with spike, neither
  touches it: they are there bound to ACE2, which is what spike holds.
- **B2M with the viral peptides.** B2M shares entries with the spike, N and
  nsp13 epitopes, but it is the HLA-B heavy chain that holds the peptide in its
  groove, and HLA-B is in the table above.
- **ETV6 and nsp14.** Its {etv6?.fusions.length} shared entries are
  crystallisation fusions, one chain containing both sequences, so there is no
  interface to measure.

## How it is counted

- The viral proteins are the {v.summary['viral proteins']} reviewed SARS-CoV-2
  entries in UniProt. The two replicase polyproteins are named by the mature
  protein their contacted residues fall in, from UniProt's chain features, so a
  contact in pp1ab becomes nsp5 or nsp13.
- SIFTS gives the PDB entries holding both proteins. Up to four are measured per
  pair, fewest chains first, and the one with the most contacts is reported.
  Within an entry the interface is one chain of each protein, the pair that
  contacts the other most: a crystal often holds several copies, and the copies
  do not pair up in order. A residue is in contact when a heavy atom is within
  5 Å of the partner, the same rule as the rest of the explorer.
- A chain containing both sequences is a fusion construct, not a complex, and is
  left out. So are the {v.reagents.length} human proteins present as structural
  reagents ({listed(v.reagents.map(r => r.gene))}): a nanodisc scaffold or a
  trimerisation domain touching the viral protein says nothing about infection.
- Nothing here is evidence about COVID-19 susceptibility. These are variants
  classified for Mendelian disease that happen to lie where a viral protein
  binds. Several of the structures are peptide complexes, which show one side of
  the interaction only.
