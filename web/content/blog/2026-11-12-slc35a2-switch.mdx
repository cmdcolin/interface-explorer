The explorer places ClinVar variants on the surfaces two proteins share.
SLC35A2, the transporter that imports UDP-galactose into the Golgi for
glycosylation, has {m.pathogenicTotal} pathogenic missense variants in ClinVar
(SLC35A2-CDG), but AlphaFold DB predicts no complex for it, so it has no
gene page here. A transporter works by switching between states,
though, and the residues that move when it switches form a surface the protein
makes with itself, present in one state and absent in the other.

Jindrich Lnenicka, on the BioHackathon Europe 2026 team, simulated SLC35A2 in
GROMACS in both of its open states, lumen-open and cytosol-open, three
replicas each. StarMD, the tool they built to read the runs, clusters the
frames into conformers by their Cα distance maps and ranks what tells two
conformers apart: the residue pairs whose distance changes most, and the
residues whose Foldseek 3Di letter (the local shape of the backbone) changes.
The top pair, {m.top?.from}–{m.top?.to}, goes from {m.top?.c1} nm apart in one
conformer to {m.top?.c2} nm in the other.

## Conservation, AlphaMissense and ClinVar at the switch residues

We took StarMD's {m.pairCount} top-ranked pairs and {m.localCount} top 3Di
residues and asked of them what the explorer asks of an interface. The{' '}
{m.tested} switch residues AlphaFold models confidently (pLDDT ≥ {plddt}) are
compared with random sets of the same size, buried to the same degree, drawn
from the {m.ordered} confident residues. Buried residues are conserved anyway,
so burial has to be matched.

- **Conservation:** vertebrate phyloP averages {m.phylop} on the switch
  residues against {m.phylopBackground} on the rest (p {m.phylopP}). They are
  no more buried than average ({m.burial} neighbours within 10 Å, against{' '}
  {m.burialBackground}), so the matching changes little.
- **AlphaMissense** does not single them out: {m.alphaMissense} against{' '}
  {m.alphaMissenseBackground} (p {m.alphaMissenseP}). It scores most of the
  transmembrane core high already, and it scores a single structure, so it
  cannot see the switch.
- **ClinVar:** {m.pathogenicOn} of the {m.pathogenicTotal} pathogenic variants
  sits on a switch residue (<Html html={m.pathogenicLinks} />; p {m.pathogenicP}), and{' '}
  {m.pathogenicNearby} within one residue of one (p {m.pathogenicNearbyP}). The
  switch residues hold {m.uncertainOn} residues with an uncertain variant
  (p {m.uncertainP}).

The residues that move are more conserved than their burial explains, and
ClinVar has little evidence about what changing them does.

## The variants worth a look

The uncertain variants on switch residues are{' '}
<Html html={m.uncertainLinks} />. Most of the top-ranked pairs run from{' '}
{m.hub} to the stretch {m.stretch}, which holds {m.stretchUncertain} of them.
{m.hub} also pairs with R314, L315 and F316. L315P is the one pathogenic variant
on a switch residue, and R314C, uncertain, is next to it. A substitution here
would most plausibly jam the transporter in one state, so these are the ones a
new simulation would test first.

SLC35A2's <a href={isoformHref('SLC35A2')}>isoform page</a> has its variants
along the protein and in the genome.

## What this does not show

- It is one protein, and the residue lists are StarMD's top-ranked rows, not
  every residue that moves.
- We don't label which conformer is lumen-open and which cytosol-open; the
  switch analysis doesn't depend on it.
- {m.hub}, at the centre of most pairs, is modelled with pLDDT{' '}
  {Math.round(m.hubPlddt)}, below the cut, so it is left out of the tests.
