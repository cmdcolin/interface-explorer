UCSC's alignment of 100 vertebrate genomes to hg38, from human to lamprey, gives
every base a phyloP score: the evidence, as −log₁₀ p, that it has changed more
slowly than neutral DNA would, negative where it has changed faster. We read it
at every codon of the disease proteins with a predicted complex,
{c.variants.scored.toLocaleString()} classified ClinVar missense variants among
them, and put two questions to it.

## Conservation alone predicts pathogenicity

Of the variants classified pathogenic or benign, the share that are pathogenic,

That raises a fair objection to the
[interface finding](../2026-11-12-disease-variants-cluster/): if interfaces are
conserved, being at one might be conservation under another name.

## Interfaces still matter at matched conservation

Here pathogenic and benign variants are compared only within the same quarter
of conservation, protein by protein.

<Html html={forestPlot(conservationRows(s))} />

Over all residues the odds ratio goes from {odds(all.unmatched).toFixed(1)}× to
{odds(all.matched).toFixed(1)}×; on the folded surface, the comparison that sets
folding aside, from {odds(surface.unmatched).toFixed(1)}× to
**{odds(surface.matched).toFixed(1)}×**. Conservation accounts for part of the
interface signal, not most of it.

Mantel–Haenszel odds ratios with 95% intervals from resampling proteins. The

variant, so the matched rows rest on fewer variants: on the folded surface,
{counted(surface.matched).toLocaleString()} of
{counted(surface.unmatched).toLocaleString()}.

## Predicted interfaces are more conserved than the rest of the surface

For each confident complex, we compared the conservation of its interface
residues (the mean phyloP of each codon) with that of the protein's other
folded, surface-exposed residues at no confident interface, matched for
exposure and pLDDT. The interface is more conserved in
**{percent(r.all['interface more conserved'], r.all.complexes)}** of
{r.all.complexes.toLocaleString()} complexes, by a median
{r.all['median difference']} phyloP units.

<Html html={shareBars(conservedInterfaceRows(s), 'var(--interface)')} />

Share of complexes whose interface is more conserved, by ipSAE. Hover a row for
the counts.

That is what real interfaces would show, but it does not prove these ones are
real: a compact patch of any folded surface might beat the surface average, and
the share does not rise with confidence. A fair test would set each interface
against random surface patches of its size.

## Caveats

- phyloP measures DNA, not protein. A codon can be conserved for splicing or an
  overlapping element, and a third position free to change lowers a codon's
  mean.
- Surface residues at no confident interface may touch partners AlphaFold DB
  did not model, which makes the comparison conservative.
- Quarters of conservation are coarse: within one, interface variants may still
  sit on somewhat more conserved bases.
- phyloP summarises the whole alignment column, so reading the alignment species
  by species would add little to these questions. The phyloP track is in
  [JBrowse](../../jbrowse/) beside the interface tracks.
