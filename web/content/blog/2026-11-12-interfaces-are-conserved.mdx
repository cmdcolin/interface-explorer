UCSC's alignment of 100 vertebrate genomes to hg38, from human to lamprey, gives
every base a phyloP score: the evidence, as −log₁₀ p, that it has changed more
slowly than neutral DNA would, negative where it has changed faster. We read it
at every codon of the disease proteins with a predicted complex,
{c.variants.scored.toLocaleString()} classified ClinVar missense variants among
them, and put two questions to it.

## Conservation alone predicts pathogenicity

That raises a fair objection to the
[interface finding](../2026-11-12-disease-variants-cluster/): if interfaces are
conserved, being at one might be conservation under another name.

## Interfaces still matter at matched conservation

Here pathogenic and benign variants are compared only within the same quarter

## Predicted interfaces are more conserved than the rest of the surface

For each confident complex, we compared the conservation of its interface

## Not just any patch

Any compact patch of folded surface might stand out like that. So we ranked
each interface among patches of its size from the same protein's folded surface
at no confident interface: one patch centred on each such residue, made of the
residues nearest it in the AlphaFold DB model of the protein alone, and each
scored against the rest of the surface as the interface is. To learn what
chance gives, we ranked a random one of those patches the same way, standing in
for the interface.

The interface beats the median patch in
**{percent(pa['above the median patch'].interface, pa.complexes)}** of
{pa.complexes.toLocaleString()} complexes, against
{percent(pa['above the median patch'].fake, pa.complexes)} for the stand-in, and
beats 95% of its patches in
{percent(pa['above 95% of patches'].interface, pa.complexes)}, against
{percent(pa['above 95% of patches'].fake, pa.complexes)}.

<Html html={shareBars(patchRows(s), 'var(--interface)')} />

Share of interfaces more conserved than the median patch of their protein's
surface, by ipSAE, and the same for a random patch ranked in an interface's
place. Hover a row for the counts.

The share rises with confidence, as it would if the more confident predictions
are more often right.

Interfaces could still just sit in conserved domains. Ranked only among patches
centred within 25 Å of its middle, the interface beats the median one in
{percent(near['above the median patch'].interface, near.complexes)} of
{near.complexes.toLocaleString()} complexes, against
{percent(near['above the median patch'].fake, near.complexes)} for a stand-in
ranked among the patches around it.

So conservation marks most predicted interfaces as functional surface, which a
misplaced interface would not be. It cannot vouch for the partner: AlphaFold
reads the same alignments, and could dock a wrong partner on a real conserved
site, such as a paralog's. The test needs room for the patches and a stand-in,
so it covers {pa.complexes.toLocaleString()} of the
{r.all.complexes.toLocaleString()} complexes.

## Caveats

- phyloP measures DNA, not protein. A codon can be conserved for splicing or an
  overlapping element, and a third position free to change lowers a codon's
  mean.
- Surface residues at no confident interface may touch partners AlphaFold DB
  did not model, which makes the comparisons conservative.
- Patches are the residues nearest a point, so they are rounder than many real
  interfaces.
- Quarters of conservation are coarse: within one, interface variants may still
  sit on somewhat more conserved bases.
- phyloP summarises the whole alignment column, so reading the alignment species
  by species would add little to these questions. The phyloP track is in
  [JBrowse](../../jbrowse/) beside the interface tracks.
